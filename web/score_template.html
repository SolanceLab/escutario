What moved you · choose any
Arrival. Where the arrangement changes most: parts coming in or taking over. Taller means a bigger shift. A hollow one is the splitter relabelling the same sound (piano filed as "other" at the start of the mashup), not a new part.
Breath. The breath mark from a written score, placed where an inhale was heard. Solid means confident, hollow means likely. Not yet checked by ear.
The voice. The sung pitch, higher is higher. Both singers share this lane until the voices are separated.
Voices. How many vocal lines sound at once. One bar is a single voice, a second bar means two, a third means three layered. Counted from notes heard at the same time, so a strong echo can add a bar.
Held note. A note sustained for almost a second or more. Dashed ones are heard through the layered voices, so a backing voice can be among them.
Riff. A cengkok: one voice running through neighbouring notes, circling and turning. Faint means likely, solid means clear. Counted as one voice, not two.
Crying fall. The pitch dropping away at the end of a phrase.
Loudness. The width of the voice's ribbon is how loud it sings. The faint line is the whole song.
Texture. Bars show the 2–4 kHz ring of a projected voice per phrase, taller rings brighter. Gold hatching marks strong grit (roughness while loud) where one voice sings; grit is not yet calibrated.
Voice change. A leap too large for one singer. Usually the other voice taking over, or the pitch tracker slipping an octave. Set aside, not counted.
Instruments. The song split into six tracks. The brighter the strip, the louder that part plays.

What this can't tell you yet Both songs are duets, so the two voices still share one track, which is why range and time-at-the-top are marked low confidence. Breath marks haven't been checked against a human ear. Readings marked weak are guesses. Every measurement here is Escutário's own; it began from Maii's Attune.
